Compatibility is an arrow, not an equals sign.

A richer encoder can sometimes speak perfectly to an old model. That does not mean the old representation can speak back.

September 3, 2026Han Joon Byun

I began Day 11 looking for another model improvement. A native-geometry search reached a locked KILL: its best method gained only 0.240% at the median across 20 final cells. A second idea—bridging tabular foundation-model latents to language models—also stopped at its gates.

The result that survived was quieter and more useful. A deployed model should not have to be retrained every time its feature encoder is refitted, widened, or given a new vocabulary. But the replacement cannot merely output a tensor of the same shape. It must speak the old model's language.

An encoder upgrade is not just a new model. It is a change to an interface.

INSIGHT 01

Compatibility belongs to a request, not a pair. The question is not whether two encoders are “equivalent.” It is whether the new service can reproduce the response one particular frozen consumer needs.

The arrow only points one way

Suppose version 2 keeps every old basis function and adds new ones. Going backward is easy: select the old coordinates and ignore the additions. Going forward is impossible in general: the smaller old representation cannot invent signals it never stored.

Linear algebra makes the rule precise. Put the new encoder's responses in one matrix and the old responses in another. An exact backward adapter exists precisely when every old response column lies inside the span of the new responses. The relationship is containment, not equality—and containment has a direction.

INSIGHT 02

More expressive can still be backward-compatible. Version 2 does not need to equal version 1. It only needs to contain the old response language well enough to serve the old consumer.

Seven clean upgrades—and one important trap

Seven structured migration families passed the exact backward tests: rotations, permutations, affine changes, nested RBF and PLE expansions, and categorical evolution. Across 120 numerical feature audits, including extrapolation and missingness, the largest coordinate residual was 1.78 × 10−15.

This counterexample changed the language of the result. A finite response test can certify a finite response set. Function-level guarantees require additional structure; categorical guarantees may cover only the old support. “Exact” is incomplete unless it says where.

Safety means being willing to keep the old system

Real refits were not always exact. Without an adapter, those changes raised relative loss by a median 5.78%. Approximate adapters often repaired much of the damage, but the specialized method did not clearly beat ordinary blockwise least squares. So the certificate had to answer a harder question: when is a hot-swap safe enough to attempt?

Thirteen percent coverage is not a victory lap. It means the gate abstained most of the time. But that is the point of a deployment contract: uncertainty must become an explicit branch, not a silent prediction shift.

INSIGHT 03

Abstention is part of correctness. A compatibility layer that cannot prove a safe translation should preserve the old service or request retraining—not manufacture a green checkmark.

Ask whether the new service can satisfy the old consumer, not whether two encoders look equal.

⊂Scope

State whether a guarantee covers finite responses, a full domain, or only old states.

⊘Refusal

When evidence is insufficient, keep v1 or retrain.

Day 11 conclusion. A model pipeline needs a representation ABI. Tensor shape is only syntax; compatibility is the directional promise that a new producer can still deliver the responses an old consumer understands—and the promise must include when to refuse.
