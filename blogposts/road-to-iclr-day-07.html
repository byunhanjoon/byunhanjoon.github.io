The average is
part of the model.

A neural ensemble is not merely “several runs.” It is a choice about which harmless changes and random events count as the same predictor.

August 30, 2026Han Joon ByunPaper draft ↗

Day 5 proposed averaging away accidents such as column order, category IDs, and training seeds. Day 7 asked the harder question: does that idea survive fresh independent randomness, four neural architectures, realistic training, and an explicit audit of what is actually being averaged?

The answer. Yes at small budget—but only for a coupled, explicitly declared average. Balance the schema while assigning unrelated fresh seeds, and the benefit disappears.

An ensemble is a question

Suppose two tables differ only by a column permutation or a bijective renaming of category IDs. Semantically, they describe the same task. Computationally, the training pipeline may follow a different path.

This sounds like notation, but it changes the experiment. A canonical seed ensemble, a schema-plus-fresh-seed ensemble, and a finite coupled schema–initialization–order ensemble need not converge to the same prediction. In the final audit, the canonical and schema-averaged targets were distinguishable beyond Monte Carlo noise in 10 of 144 neural cells.

Rule 1Never hide a changed target inside a variance-reduction claim.

Balance pairs, not just lists

Random sampling can visit some factor pairs repeatedly and miss others. A strength-2 cover arranges the runs so every pair is represented evenly. If prediction variation is mostly made of one-factor and two-factor effects, their errors cancel.

The prediction field can be decomposed into main effects, pair interactions, triples, and higher-order pieces. This is functional ANOVA in prediction space. A strength-2 design deletes the first two layers from its integration error—not approximately, but algebraically.

Design strength is a spectral filter, not a universal ranking rule.

The tempting conclusion would be “schema balance beats seed ensembling.” That conclusion is false. When the schema was balanced but every run received an unrelated fresh RNG, the method won only 5 of 144 cells and lost 7.0% on average.

The first number is the clean mechanism test: every ablation estimates the same finite quotient. Jointly balancing schema, initialization, and order beats balancing none, any one factor, or any pair. The interaction—not the word “orthogonal”—is doing the work.

Three boundaries worth keeping

mean cover-to-SRS residual ratio at convergence: effectively no advantage.

03 · SELECTIONVariance is not shift19 / 36

exact validation and test winners agreed in only 19 partitions; test regret barely changed.

Exact function matching sharpened the second boundary. It almost erased schema effects for MLP and ResNet, while FT-Transformer and TabM retained small architecture-specific paths that also shrank with training. Equivalent representations do not universally force different converged functions.

Day 7 conclusion. An ensemble is a numerical integral over a declared learning pipeline. OrbitCover can estimate that integral efficiently when low-order schema and training interactions are deliberately coupled. It is not a universal better ensemble, and it does not solve validation-to-test shift.
01Define the average

“Five models” is not an estimand. State which representations and random events belong to the predictor.

02Couple what interacts

Balance the joint pipeline, not isolated factor lists. Otherwise the cancellation guarantee misses the variation.

03Publish the boundary

Target shift, independent failure, and convergence failure are part of the result—not appendix debris.
