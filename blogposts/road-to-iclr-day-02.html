Road to ICLR · Day 2 / 30

Same information,
different path

An identity view did not tell the model anything that fine-grained PLE could not. It changed how easily the model could use what it already knew.

August 25, 2026 Han Joon Byun Initial experiments ↗ Frozen follow-up ↗

Day 1 ended with an oddly specific result. Piecewise-linear encoding (PLE)[1] was a strong default for numerical features, but Adult improved when capital gain and capital loss also received exact-value identity views. Black Friday showed a smaller version of the same effect. Diamond and California showed none.

The tempting explanation was point masses. Adult has many zeros: 91.7% of capital gains and 95.3% of capital losses are exactly zero. Perhaps three indicator variables—gain is zero, loss is zero, hours are forty—would explain the entire result.

They did not. The full identity view remained better, hours-per-week was a false positive, and the useful columns were stable across new splits and three neural backbones. That left a more interesting question:

Day 2 question. If PLE and identity can represent the same exact states, why does giving the model both make learning easier?

Two coordinate systems for the same states

PLE represents a value through cumulative, overlapping coordinates. Nearby values share most of their activation. Identity gives each supported value its own coordinate. Click through the five states below.

For a column with K supported values, centered one-hot identity has rank K − 1. Fine-grained cumulative PLE has the same rank on those values. A first linear layer can translate between them. The singleton identity view therefore adds neither information nor finite-support expressivity.

But equivalent function classes do not imply equivalent learning. Weight decay penalizes coordinates, not abstract functions. Gradient descent follows the geometry of the chosen basis. A state-local exception may require a long, oscillating combination of cumulative PLE coordinates and only one identity coordinate.

Looking only where the smooth model fails

I replaced Day 1’s variance heuristic with a stricter residual test. First, a smooth model uses schema information and 128-bin PLE. Then, on held-out training folds, I ask whether its remaining errors still align with an exact state.

→
2held-out residualwhat PLE missed
→
3atomic viewonly if 5/5 folds improve

A singleton state s receives the shrunk correction

θ̂s = Σi: xᵢ=s rᵢ / (ns + 20)

so an unsupported state returns zero and a rare state stays close to the PLE prediction. Labels choose the structure using training folds; the neural model receives only a target-free, train-vocabulary one-hot view. I capped singleton cardinality at 128, pair cardinality at 512, and the total addition at 512 states. Every neural comparison was matched to the PLE baseline’s parameter count.

Additive exceptions are not interactions

Black Friday raised a second issue. If occupation and product category each have their own exact effect, a joint lookup may look useful even when no interaction exists. A pair should receive credit only for what remains after both main effects.

Adult is the additive case: capital gain and capital loss are useful separately. Black Friday is the interactive case: after an occupation main effect, three product-category pairs still leave stable joint residuals. This distinction turned an empirical trick into a testable decomposition:

prediction = smooth background + atomic main effects + pure atomic interactions

The gain is partly geometry

The selected Adult columns make the change of basis visible. On their observed states, PLE and identity reconstruct the same residual function to numerical precision. Their geometry is very different.

For capital gain, the PLE basis has condition number 10,738; identity has 46. The minimum coefficient norm needed to express the observed state residual is 23.05 with PLE and 3.01 with identity. Capital loss shows the same direction, though less dramatically: condition numbers 474 and 35.

This does not prove that conditioning alone causes the neural gain. It does rule out the simplest expressivity story. Identity appears to act as a shortcut—a redundant coordinate system in which certain exceptions are cheaper to reach.

Three successes—and a necessary failure

I tested the fixed view with MLP, ResNet, and TabM backbones, following the spirit of TabPack’s cross-model tabular evaluation.[2] The bars below show paired test improvement over parameter-matched PLE.

Adult is robust but not a clean victory for selection: adding identity to every eligible singleton still gains about 1.05 AUC points, versus 1.16 for the selected two. The representation matters more than the selector there.

Miami is more diagnostic. The frozen residual rule selected one pair on training data. On the untouched official test split, that pair reduces RMSE by 1.76% for MLP and 1.63% for ResNet. Cardinality-matched random pairs do worse, and adding every singleton identity hurts both models.

The null control was reassuring: after permuting labels twenty times on Adult and twenty times on Black Friday, the selector made zero discoveries in forty attempts.

The broad method did not survive

A useful ICLR method cannot live on three datasets. Before opening a new TabArena block,[3] I froze a simple gate: at least two of six untouched datasets had to select a nonempty structure. Only Miami did.

Day 2 conclusion. Exact-state views are a real, interpretable shortcut on a few datasets—not a broadly supported numerical encoder. The deeper result is that information-equivalent bases can make the same function much easier or harder to learn.

That changes the research direction. The question is no longer “when should I add identity on top of PLE?” It is “how sensitive are tabular learners to information-equivalent schemas, why, and can training become robust to that equivalence class?”

Singleton identity gives a controlled reparameterization example: same information, same finite-support function class, different geometry. Pure crossed identity gives the complementary example: genuinely new interaction space. Together they are more useful as a mechanism inside a broader schema-sensitivity study than as a standalone method.
