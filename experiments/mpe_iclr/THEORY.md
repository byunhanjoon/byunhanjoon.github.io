# Theory of Metric Partition Embeddings

This document separates deterministic approximation facts from claims about
optimization or statistical generalization. Theorems 1–6 and Proposition 7 are
proved below. Their synthetic checks were generated by `theory_suite.py` from
the frozen `THEORY_PLAN.md`; raw CSV and Parquet files are under `raw/`.

## 1. Definitions and assumptions

Let `(X,d)` be a metric or pseudometric space and let
`L=(l_1,...,l_m)` be distinct landmarks selected from training states. For a
bandwidth `h>0` and nonnegative kernel `kappa`, define

```text
a_j^d(x) = kappa(d(x,l_j)/h),
Z_d(x)   = sum_j a_j^d(x),
w_j^d(x) = a_j^d(x)/Z_d(x).
```

Every statement about normalized weights assumes `Z_d(x)>0`. This is automatic
for the Gaussian kernel `kappa(r)=exp(-r^2/2)`. It is not automatic for compact
support. Given landmark tokens `v_j in R^D`, the tokenizer is

```text
MPE_d(x) = sum_j w_j^d(x) v_j.
```

For landmark output values `b_j in R^q`, the corresponding partition
interpolant is `f_hat(x)=sum_j w_j^d(x)b_j`. Define its weighted metric radius

```text
R_w(x)=sum_j w_j^d(x)d(x,l_j).
```

## 2. Theorem 1 — exact chart/relabeling invariance

**Statement.** Let `pi:X->X'` be a bijection. Transport the metric by
`d'(pi(x),pi(y))=d(x,y)` and the landmarks by `l'_j=pi(l_j)`. If landmark token
`v'_j` is identified with `v_j`, then

```text
w_j^{d'}(pi(x))=w_j^d(x)
and
MPE_{d'}(pi(x))=MPE_d(x).
```

**Proof.** For every j,

```text
a_j^{d'}(pi(x))
= kappa(d'(pi(x),pi(l_j))/h)
= kappa(d(x,l_j)/h)
= a_j^d(x).
```

Summing the equal terms proves `Z_{d'}(pi(x))=Z_d(x)`. Division gives equality
of weights. Finally,

```text
MPE_{d'}(pi(x))
= sum_j w_j^{d'}(pi(x))v'_j
= sum_j w_j^d(x)v_j
= MPE_d(x).
```

No distributional or optimization assumption is used. The result says storage
codes are charts when metric metadata is transported; it does not say MPE is
more predictive than another invariant method. ∎

**Validation.** Across 32 relabelings of each of nine synthetic discrete
representations (288 checks), the maximum aligned weight and representation
difference in float64 was exactly `0.0`, below the frozen `1e-7` tolerance.
Real-field checks are reported separately after data acquisition.

## 3. Theorem 2 — partition-of-unity interpolation bound

**Statement.** Let `f:X->R^q` be `L_f`-Lipschitz under d, and let landmark
approximations `b_j` be arbitrary. Then

```text
||f_hat(x)-f(x)||
<= L_f sum_j w_j(x)d(x,l_j)
   + sum_j w_j(x)||b_j-f(l_j)||.
```

If `||b_j-f(l_j)||<=epsilon` for every j, then

```text
||f_hat(x)-f(x)|| <= L_f R_w(x)+epsilon.
```

For a compact-support kernel whose active landmarks all lie within distance h,
the bound becomes `L_f h+epsilon`.

**Proof.** Add and subtract `sum_j w_j(x)f(l_j)`, then apply the triangle
inequality:

```text
||sum_j w_j b_j - f(x)||
<= ||sum_j w_j(b_j-f(l_j))||
   + ||sum_j w_j(f(l_j)-f(x))||.
```

Weights are nonnegative and sum to one, so convexity of the norm and the
Lipschitz property give

```text
<= sum_j w_j||b_j-f(l_j)||
   + sum_j w_j L_f d(x,l_j).
```

The uniform landmark-error conclusion uses `sum_j w_j=1`. If every positive
weight has `d(x,l_j)<=h`, then `R_w(x)<=h`. ∎

This theorem proves that the MPE function class contains a controlled
interpolant. It does not prove that gradient descent learns landmark values,
that a finite sample estimates them accurately, or that a full network
generalizes.

**Validation.** All 324 smooth synthetic cells (nine spaces, four intended-
smooth targets, three landmark budgets, three seeds) satisfied the empirical
bound; the maximum numerical violation was `0.0`. Across the controlled
support-gap rows, weighted radius and absolute interpolation error had Spearman
rho `0.421`. The relationship is positive but far from deterministic, as the
bound permits. Discontinuous, random, and metric-misaligned targets remain in
raw results and are labeled as failures of the intended smoothness mechanism.

## 4. Theorem 3 — linear-head realizability

**Statement.** Put desired landmark outputs in the rows of
`B in R^(m x q)`. If token dimension `D>=rank(B)`, there exist
`V in R^(m x D)` and a linear head `H in R^(D x q)` such that

```text
MPE(x)H = w(x)B
```

for every x. In particular, `D>=q` is sufficient.

**Proof.** Let `r=rank(B)` and choose any rank factorization
`B=V_r H_r`, with `V_r in R^(m x r)` and `H_r in R^(r x q)`. Because `D>=r`,
pad `V_r` with `D-r` zero columns and `H_r` with `D-r` zero rows, giving V and
H. Then

```text
MPE(x)H = (w(x)V)H = w(x)(VH)=w(x)B.
```

When `D>=q`, the direct construction `V=[B,0]` and a coordinate-selecting H is
sufficient. If `rank(B)>D`, every product VH has rank at most D, so exact
realization is impossible in general. A downstream bias adds an affine constant
but does not change the rank condition for the varying part. ∎

**Validation.** Six constructive matrix cases below, at, and above `D=32`
behaved exactly as the rank statement predicts; every predicted-exact case had
relative reconstruction error below `1e-10`, and rank-40 did not.

## 5. Theorem 4 — equality-metric impossibility

**Statement.** Let `d_eq(x,y)=0` if `x=y` and 1 otherwise. If x and x' are both
absent from the landmark set, then every positive-normalizer metric-only MPE
has

```text
w(x)=w(x')
and
MPE(x)=MPE(x').
```

**Proof.** Since neither query equals a landmark,
`d_eq(x,l_j)=d_eq(x',l_j)=1` for every j. Thus their affinity vectors are
componentwise equal, their normalizers are equal, and their weights are equal.
Multiplication by the same landmark-token table preserves equality. ∎

For Gaussian MPE, every unseen weight is `1/m`. A triangular kernel with
`h<=1` has zero normalizer and is undefined; that is an implementation error,
not a way around the impossibility.

**Validation.** The synthetic equality space gave maximum unseen weight
difference `0.0`. Real nominal controls test whether optimization/capacity
confounds produce apparent gains despite this information-theoretic collapse.

## 6. Theorem 5 — stability to metric perturbation

**Statement.** Suppose for a fixed query and landmarks that

```text
|d(x,l_j)-d_tilde(x,l_j)| <= delta
```

for every j. Assume kappa is `L_kappa`-Lipschitz on the relevant nonnegative
arguments, `Z_d(x)>=z0>0`, and `||v_j||<=V`. Then

```text
||MPE_d(x)-MPE_dtilde(x)||
<= 2 V m L_kappa delta/(h z0).
```

**Proof.** Kernel Lipschitzness gives

```text
|a_j-a_tilde_j| <= L_kappa delta/h.
```

Let `A=sum_j|a_j-a_tilde_j|`; then
`A<=m L_kappa delta/h` and `|Z-Z_tilde|<=A`. Decompose using denominator Z:

```text
sum_j |a_j/Z-a_tilde_j/Z_tilde|
<= sum_j |a_j-a_tilde_j|/Z
   + sum_j a_tilde_j |1/Z-1/Z_tilde|
= A/Z + |Z-Z_tilde|/Z
<= 2A/z0.
```

Therefore

```text
||sum_j(w_j-w_tilde_j)v_j||
<= sum_j |w_j-w_tilde_j| ||v_j||
<= V ||w-w_tilde||_1
<= 2 V m L_kappa delta/(h z0).
```

Only the displayed Z needs the lower bound for this orientation; requiring both
Z and Z-tilde to exceed z0 is a symmetric sufficient assumption used by the
numerical audit. For the Gaussian on `[0,infinity)`, the derivative magnitude
`r exp(-r^2/2)` is maximized at r=1, so `L_kappa=exp(-1/2)`. ∎

The constant is worst-case and can be loose. It bounds representation change,
not target loss or monotonic performance under discrete state permutations.

**Validation.** All six continuous perturbation magnitudes passed; the largest
observed bound ratio was `0.0271`. Full state association corruption worsened
smooth-target test MSE in 24/27 space-seed cells, not all 27. This preserves the
mandate's warning that predictive degradation need not be monotone.

## 7. Theorem 6 — coverage and metric complexity

**Statement (compact support).** If L is an r-cover of the relevant support,
and a compact kernel uses bandwidth `h>=r`, has at least one active landmark for
every query, and assigns positive weight only within distance h, then the exact-
landmark interpolant obeys

```text
||f_hat(x)-f(x)|| <= L_f h.
```

With landmark error epsilon, add epsilon. A minimum r-cover has cardinality
`N(X,r)`, the metric covering number; thus a sufficient landmark budget is
governed by coverage, not the number or magnitude of storage codes.

**Proof.** Coverage and `h>=r` ensure an active landmark exists. Compact support
ensures every active landmark is within h, so `R_w(x)<=h`. Apply Theorem 2. The
covering-number statement is its definition: `N(X,r)` is the minimum size of an
r-cover. ∎

**Gaussian tail statement.** Suppose additionally that the support diameter is
at most Delta, Gaussian/monotone kappa is used, the nearest landmark is within
r, and `rho>=r`. Split landmarks into those within rho and those beyond it. The
near contribution to `R_w` is at most rho. The denominator is at least
`kappa(r/h)`, while each of at most `m-1` far affinities is at most
`kappa(rho/h)`. Hence

```text
R_w(x)
<= rho + Delta (m-1) kappa(rho/h)/kappa(r/h).
```

Inserting this into Theorem 2 yields a valid Gaussian-tail approximation bound.
It is not an asymptotic rate. Rates require further covering-growth, doubling,
or dimension assumptions.

**Validation.** All 567 coverage/target cells had finite cover radii and raw
state count, cover radius, and error are retained for the real/synthetic
complexity analysis. This confirms computability, not a universal empirical
rate.

## 8. Proposition 7 — the interval special case

**Statement.** Let line landmarks be uniformly spaced by Delta, use
`kappa(t)=max(0,1-t)`, and set `h=Delta`. For x between adjacent landmarks
`l_i<=x<=l_(i+1)`, MPE is ordinary linear interpolation:

```text
w_i(x)     = (l_(i+1)-x)/Delta,
w_(i+1)(x) = (x-l_i)/Delta,
```

with all other weights zero.

**Proof.** Every nonadjacent landmark is at distance at least Delta and has zero
affinity. The two adjacent affinities are

```text
1-(x-l_i)/Delta
and
1-(l_(i+1)-x)/Delta.
```

They sum to one, so normalization does not alter them. Their weighted token sum
is the usual barycentric/piecewise-linear interpolant. ∎

**Validation.** At 10,001 points, the maximum difference from an independently
coded linear interpolant was `2.998e-15`. MPE does not invent piecewise-linear
numerical interpolation; this special case is directly related to PLE.

## 9. Proposition — relationship to Similarity Encoding

Let normalized Similarity Encoding expose `s(x)=w(x) in R^m`. An MPE token map
followed by a linear first backbone layer B produces

```text
s(x) V B.
```

A model taking s directly with first layer `C=VB` produces exactly the same
preactivation. Conversely, when `m=D` and V is allowed full rank, MPE can choose
V as the identity and reproduce any direct similarity first layer. When D is
smaller, MPE imposes a rank-D factorization/bottleneck; it adds no new metric
information. Nonlinearities between V and B or optimization regularization can
change training behavior, but not the source similarities.

This algebra is why the same-metric Similarity Encoding and Nyström showdowns
are decisive for method novelty.

## 10. What these results establish—and do not

The deterministic claims establish chart invariance, a partition interpolation
bound, architectural realizability under rank assumptions, equality no-free-
lunch, a worst-case perturbation bound, and coverage-based complexity. The
synthetic suite validates their arithmetic and shows the expected smooth-target
mechanism more often than not under corruption. It does not establish that
real conditional targets are smooth, that supplied metadata are relevant, that
MPE learns landmark values reliably, or that learned token mixing improves on
classical similarity/kernel coordinates. Those are exclusively empirical
questions for the prospectively frozen real panel.
