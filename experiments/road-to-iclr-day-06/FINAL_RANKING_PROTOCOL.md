## Weighted score

Each direction receives an integer 0–5 on five dimensions.  The weighted total
is scaled to 100:

| Dimension | Weight | A score of 5 requires |
|---|---:|---|
| novelty after closest-work subtraction | 25% | a contribution not already contained in the audited closest construction, with a clear new object or consequence |
| mathematical/theoretical substance | 20% | a nontrivial formal result that predicts the tested intervention or boundary, with assumptions and failure cases |
| prospective empirical strength | 25% | completed frozen gates, replication at the dataset unit, broad architecture/data scope, and direct integrity evidence |
| practical/scientific utility | 20% | reliable improvement in a consequential outcome, decision, robustness property, or compute cost—not merely a measurable effect |
| execution and paper feasibility | 10% | reproducible implementation, manageable cost, clear narrative, and credible additional-validation path |

`total = 20 * sum(weight_d * score_d)`, where weights sum to one.

## Evidence caps and penalties

- A direction whose defining frozen primary hypothesis fails is discarded in
  that form; a modified successor is scored only on its own pre-outcome gates.
- Purely post-hoc evidence caps prospective empirical strength at 2/5.
- Evidence on only three datasets caps prospective empirical strength at 3/5;
  repeated seeds/views do not increase the dataset count.
- A close literature collision caps novelty at 3/5 unless a distinct theorem,
  intervention, or downstream consequence survives prospective gates.
- A numerical reproducibility intervention with no accuracy, cost, reliability,
  or decision benefit caps utility at 3/5.
- An active or incomplete gate counts as missing evidence, not a pass.
- Exact within-tensor repetitions are not treated as independent datasets.
- A failed result is scientifically valuable but does not receive a success
  bonus.  Negative evidence can improve the boundary/clarity component of the
  theory score only when it genuinely narrows the claim.

## Tie-breaks

Ties in weighted score are broken in order by:

1. higher novelty after subtraction;
2. stronger dataset-unit prospective evidence;
3. higher practical utility;
4. lower remaining cost to a credible ICLR submission.
